Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to charge an electric vehicle at home?

The average cost to charge an EV at home is $0.04-$0.05 per mile, compared to $0.12-$0.15 per mile for gas. For a typical driver (12,000 miles/year), that's $480-$600 for electricity vs $1,440-$1,800 for gas, saving $960-$1,200 annually. Your actual cost depends on your local electricity rate and vehicle efficiency.

How long does a home EV charger take to pay for itself?

A Level 2 home charger (equipment + installation: $1,000-$3,000) typically pays for itself in 1-3 years through fuel savings. Use our Home Charging ROI Calculator to find your exact payback period based on your driving habits and local electricity rates.

Are electric vehicles cheaper than gas cars over 5 years?

Yes, most EVs are cheaper over 5 years despite higher upfront costs. EVs save on fuel ($5,000-$10,000), maintenance ($2,000-$4,000), and benefit from tax credits (up to $7,500). Total 5-year savings typically range from $7,000-$15,000. See the full breakdown with our TCO Comparison Calculator.

What is the federal tax credit for electric vehicles?

The federal EV tax credit is up to $7,500 for new EVs that meet specific requirements: battery size (7+ kWh), final assembly in North America, MSRP limits ($55K for sedans, $80K for SUVs/trucks), and buyer income limits. Used EVs qualify for up to $4,000. Check IRS.gov for current eligibility.

How much does EV maintenance cost compared to gas cars?

EVs cost about 40% less to maintain than gas vehicles. No oil changes, transmission fluid, spark plugs, or exhaust systems. Brake pads last 2-3x longer due to regenerative braking. Average EV maintenance: $0.06/mile vs gas $0.10/mile. Over 5 years (60,000 miles), that's $3,600 vs $6,000—a $2,400 savings.

Can I install a Level 2 charger in my garage?

Most garages can support a Level 2 charger (240V, same as a dryer outlet). Requirements: dedicated 40-50 amp circuit, electrical panel within 25 feet (longer runs cost more), and proper grounding. Installation typically costs $500-$2,000 depending on your electrical panel's capacity and distance. Always hire a licensed electrician.

Do EVs work in cold weather?

Yes, but range decreases 10-40% in extreme cold (below 20°F) due to battery chemistry and cabin heating. Modern EVs have battery thermal management systems to minimize impact. Pre-conditioning while plugged in (warming the car before driving) helps preserve range. Most EV owners in cold climates report minimal issues for daily driving.

How long do EV batteries last?

Most EV batteries are warrantied for 8 years/100,000 miles and retain 70-80%+ capacity after that. Real-world data shows Tesla batteries retain 90% capacity after 200,000 miles. Battery degradation is gradual—you won't suddenly lose range. Replacement costs have dropped significantly ($5,000-$15,000) and continue declining.

Are EVs better for the environment?

Yes, even accounting for manufacturing and electricity generation. EVs produce 50-70% fewer lifetime emissions than gas vehicles, depending on your local electricity mix. The gap widens as the grid gets cleaner. Charging with solar panels makes EVs nearly emissions-free for operation.

Can I charge an EV at an apartment?

It depends on your landlord and building infrastructure. Options: request charger installation (may be required by law in some states), use nearby public charging (Level 2 or DC fast charging), charge at work, or look for EV-ready apartments. Many cities now require new buildings to include EV charging infrastructure.
